# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Anyone drawn to science - casual curious adults, enthusiasts, students. Used alone, in a quiet moment, usually on a phone. The common thread: genuine curiosity and a low barrier to entry. Not scientists. Not students studying for an exam. People who want to feel a small flash of wonder before their day starts.

## Product Purpose

Lumen is a daily science discovery ritual. Each day, three science facts surface across different fields - Astronomy, Physics, Biology, Chemistry, Medicine, Neuroscience - presented as "three doors." The user picks one field, and the fact is revealed with a cinematic full-bleed experience. A deeper "read more" panel is available for the curious. The daily constraint is intentional: one moment, one choice, one fact. Success looks like a person returning tomorrow.

## Brand Personality

Elegant, Intellectual, Calm. Lumen is the feeling of reading a beautiful science book by lamplight - serious without being heavy, immersive without being overwhelming, intelligent without being gatekept.

## Anti-references

- Generic science news sites: cluttered, text-heavy, ad-laden. Lumen is a designed experience, not a feed.
- Wikipedia / encyclopedia UI: dry, utilitarian, reference energy. Lumen is not a reference - it is a ritual.

## Design Principles

1. **Experience over information.** The fact is the content; the feeling of discovering it is the product. Every design decision should serve the moment of revelation, not just the legibility of text.
2. **Restraint is the feature.** One daily fact, three doors, no infinite scroll. The constraint creates anticipation. Do not design around it; design with it.
3. **Dark, not cold.** The cinematic darkness should feel like a private theatre, not a terminal. Warmth lives in the gold accent, the category colors, the prose voice - not the background.
4. **Mobile intimacy first.** This is a daily private ritual, often alone, on a phone. Design for a narrow viewport held in one hand, in low ambient light.
5. **Science is wonder, not homework.** Copy, motion, and hierarchy should evoke curiosity and delight - never obligation, anxiety, or performance.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum throughout. Reduced-motion alternatives required for all animations (film grain, framer-motion transitions, hover states). Mobile-first responsive layout. Color is always supplemented by text/shape for category identity - never color alone.
